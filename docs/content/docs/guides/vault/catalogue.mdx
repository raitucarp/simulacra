Every vault document carries a catalogue record. The record is what lets you browse by subject, find all the treatises of the early modern period, cite a book in one click, and see which entries belong together. This page explains each part of the record and where it shows up.

## The record at a glance

| Part | Fields | Where you set it |
|---|---|---|
| Description | Title, subtitle, type, creators, published date, publisher, language, identifier, pages, rights, abstract | <UiLabel>Basics</UiLabel>, <UiLabel>Publication details</UiLabel>, <UiLabel>Abstract</UiLabel> |
| Subjects | Terms from the shared subject vocabulary | <UiLabel>Classification</UiLabel> |
| Collections | Shelves of this network | <UiLabel>Classification</UiLabel> |
| Classification | Discipline, form, period, place | <UiLabel>Classification</UiLabel> |
| Provenance | Who added it, how it arrived, the text's checksum | Set for you |
| Reading | Status and progress | The reader and <UiLabel>Mark as read</UiLabel> |

See [Documents](/docs/guides/vault/documents#add-a-document) for the form itself.

## Types

| Type | For | Title in citations |
|---|---|---|
| Book | A whole book | In italics |
| Article | A paper, essay or chapter | In quotation marks |
| Note | Your own notes (the default for a new document) | In quotation marks |
| Web page | A page from the web | In quotation marks |
| Transcript | A talk, interview or recording written out | In quotation marks |
| Dataset | Tables and data | In italics |
| Media | Images, audio, video | In italics |
| Other | Anything else | In italics |

## Creators

Write creators in one line, separated by semicolons. Add the role in brackets after a name: `(translator)`, `(editor)` or `(contributor)`. A name without a role is an author.

```text
Baruch Spinoza; R. H. M. Elwes (translator)
```

Names can be written naturally ("Ludwig Wittgenstein") or inverted ("Wittgenstein, Ludwig"); citations split them into family and given names either way. A single word, such as an organisation, is kept whole.

## Identifiers

Choose a scheme chip, then type the value. Without a chosen scheme, the identifier is not saved.

| Scheme | Accepted |
|---|---|
| <UiLabel>ISBN</UiLabel> | ISBN-10 or ISBN-13 with a correct check digit; hyphens and spaces are ignored |
| <UiLabel>DOI</UiLabel> | `10.` followed by a registrant, a slash and a suffix; a `doi:` or `https://doi.org/` prefix is fine |
| <UiLabel>URL</UiLabel> | A full `http://` or `https://` address |

A DOI or URL is added to the end of every citation as a link; an ISBN is not.

## Subjects

<Screenshot id="vault-subject-tree" alt="The Subjects facet with Philosophy opened to show Ethics, Metaphysics and Philosophy of language, each with a count" size="sm" />

Subjects come from one controlled vocabulary, shared by all your networks. Each term has a preferred label, an optional scope note saying what it covers, and an optional broader term, which makes the vocabulary a tree (Philosophy › Metaphysics).

- In the form, type terms separated by commas, or click the chips of existing terms. A term you type that matches an existing label (in any letter case) reuses it instead of making a copy.
- A new term starts as a top term. The seed vocabulary shows the shape: Philosophy, with Metaphysics, Philosophy of language and Ethics under it.
- Filing a document under a narrow term also counts it under the broader ones, so choosing Philosophy in the facets finds documents filed under Metaphysics.

### Subject index

<Screenshot id="vault-subject-index" alt="The Subject index page: an illustration and title, then letters E, M and P with terms, under-notes, document counts and scope notes" />

Click <UiLabel>Subject index</UiLabel> in the vault header. Every term of the vocabulary is listed from A to Z under its initial letter (terms that do not start with a letter go under `#`, last). Each line shows the term, "under" its broader term, the number of documents filed under it, and its scope note. Terms with no documents are dimmed.

Click a term to browse every document filed under it: the search is cleared and the facets are set to that term alone. With no terms yet, the page reads <UiLabel>No subjects yet. Add some while editing a document.</UiLabel>

## Collections

Collections are named shelves of the active network, such as "Foundations". A document can sit on several. Type a name in <UiLabel>Collections</UiLabel> (or click an existing one's chip); a new name creates the collection. Every collection of the network appears in the <UiLabel>Collections</UiLabel> facet, even while empty. Collections also appear as shelves on the <UiLabel>Saved</UiLabel> side of the Knowledge page (see [Data](/docs/guides/data)).

## Classification

Four optional facets describe what kind of work a document is, independent of its subjects.

| Facet | Values |
|---|---|
| <UiLabel>Discipline</UiLabel> | General works; Philosophy & psychology; Religion; Social sciences; Language; Sciences & mathematics; Technology; Arts & recreation; Literature; History & geography |
| <UiLabel>Form</UiLabel> | Treatise, Essay, Dialogue, Aphorisms, Reference, Manual, Fiction, Poetry, Correspondence, Lecture, Report |
| <UiLabel>Period</UiLabel> | Antiquity (before 500), Middle Ages (500 to 1499), Early modern (1500 to 1799), Modern (1800 to 1944), Contemporary (1945 on) |
| <UiLabel>Place</UiLabel> | Free text: where the work comes from or what place it treats, such as "Amsterdam" |

The ten disciplines are a small built-in set of main classes, the way general library classifications divide knowledge; they deliberately follow no published scheme.

## Filing

<Screenshot id="vault-entry-filing" alt="Filed under tags: Philosophy › Metaphysics, Philosophy › Ethics, Foundations, Philosophy & psychology, Treatise, Early modern, Amsterdam; then a Contents list" />

<UiLabel>Filed under</UiLabel> on an entry shows the record as tags: each subject with its broader terms (Philosophy › Metaphysics), each collection, then the discipline, form, period and place. Click a tag to browse every document with that value. A record with none of these reads <UiLabel>Not filed yet. Edit the entry to add subjects, a collection or a discipline.</UiLabel>

## Cite a document

<Screenshot id="vault-entry-citations" alt="The Cite this card with Author–date, Notes and bibliography and Numeric citations of Ethics, each with a Copy button" />

<UiLabel>Cite this</UiLabel> formats the entry in three general styles. Click <UiLabel>Copy</UiLabel> beside one to put it on the clipboard as plain text; the button reads <UiLabel>Copied</UiLabel> until you leave the entry.

| Style | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| <UiLabel>Author–date</UiLabel> | Family, I. (Year). *Title* (I. Family, Trans.). Publisher. | Spinoza, B. (1677). *Ethics: Demonstrated in Geometrical Order* (R. H. M. Elwes, Trans.). Jan Rieuwertsz. |
| <UiLabel>Notes and bibliography</UiLabel> | Family, Given. *Title*. Translated by Given Family. Publisher, Year. | Spinoza, Baruch. *Ethics: Demonstrated in Geometrical Order*. Translated by R. H. M. Elwes. Jan Rieuwertsz, 1677. |
| <UiLabel>Numeric</UiLabel> | [1] I. Family, *Title*, Publisher, Year. | [1] B. Spinoza, *Ethics: Demonstrated in Geometrical Order*, Jan Rieuwertsz, 1677. |

- Whole works (books, datasets, media, other) are set in italics; parts (articles, notes, web pages, transcripts) in quotation marks. Italics are lost when you copy.
- Without a year, author–date writes "n.d.". Without translators, author–date names the editors ("Ed.").
- A DOI is added as a `https://doi.org/` link, a URL as it is.

## Related entries and backlinks

<Screenshot id="vault-entry-related" alt="Related entries listing Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus with 2 shared subjects, and a section reading No posts link here yet" />

<UiLabel>Related entries</UiLabel> lists up to four other documents of the network, best first, each with the reason:

| Reason | When |
|---|---|
| "2 shared subjects" | They share subjects, counting broader terms. More shared terms, relative to all their terms, rank higher. |
| <UiLabel>Same discipline</UiLabel> | No shared subject, but the same discipline. |
| <UiLabel>Close in meaning</UiLabel> | Their texts are near each other by meaning, once [search by meaning](/docs/guides/search/meaning-search) has indexed them. Used when it scores higher than the subjects. |

With nothing related: <UiLabel>Nothing related yet: file this entry under a subject to connect it.</UiLabel>

Below it, "1 post links here" lists the posts of the network that link to the document: by a `[[wikilink]]` naming its title or id, by a source chip (as agents and imports add), or because a thread was distilled into it. Each card shows the author, the day and the opening words; click it to open the post in the timeline. With none: "No posts link here yet. Write [[Ethics]] in a thought to link it."

The same link count orders the vault by <UiLabel>Most linked</UiLabel> and fills the <UiLabel>Links</UiLabel> column of the table.

## Tips and limits

- Subjects are shared by all networks, so the subject index lists every term you have made. Collections belong to one network.
- A subject or collection cannot be renamed or removed from the vault screens.
- Records imported from files keep only the fields the catalogue accepts: an unreadable date, language or identifier is left out rather than blocking the import.
